DIRECT PLANS VS REGULAR PLANS

When you invest in a mutual fund in India, the same fund often comes in two versions: a Direct Plan and a Regular Plan. The underlying portfolio — the stocks or bonds the fund holds — is identical in both. The difference is purely in how you buy it and what you pay for that.

WHAT IS A REGULAR PLAN?

A Regular Plan is bought through an intermediary — a distributor, bank, broker, or financial advisor. These middlemen earn a commission for selling the fund to you. That commission is paid out of the fund's expense ratio, which is why Regular Plans have a higher expense ratio than their Direct counterparts.

WHAT IS A DIRECT PLAN?

A Direct Plan is bought directly from the fund house (AMC) — through their website, their app, or a SEBI-registered investment adviser who charges you a fee separately. Since there's no distributor involved, there's no commission to pay. This brings the expense ratio down.

WHY DOES THIS MATTER FOR RETURNS?

Because the expense ratio is lower in a Direct Plan, a slightly higher portion of the fund's returns gets credited to your NAV each day. Over years and decades, this compounding advantage can add up to a noticeably larger corpus.

For example, if a Regular Plan has an expense ratio of 1.5% and the equivalent Direct Plan charges 0.7%, that 0.8% difference annually — while seeming small — compounds significantly over a 10-20 year horizon.

WHICH ONE IS RIGHT FOR WHOM?

Direct Plans suit investors who are comfortable doing their own research and making their own decisions, or who work with a fee-only adviser. Regular Plans may suit investors who prefer guided hand-holding from a distributor and are comfortable with the associated cost. There's no one-size-fits-all answer.
