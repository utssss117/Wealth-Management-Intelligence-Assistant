WHAT IS SEBI AND WHAT DOES IT DO IN MUTUAL FUNDS?

SEBI stands for the Securities and Exchange Board of India. It is the primary regulatory body for capital markets in India — which includes stock exchanges, brokers, listed companies, and mutual funds. SEBI was established by an Act of Parliament in 1992 and operates as an independent statutory body.

Think of SEBI as the rule-maker and watchdog for the entire investment ecosystem in India. Its core purpose is to protect investors and ensure that markets function fairly and transparently.

SEBI'S ROLE IN MUTUAL FUNDS

In the mutual fund space specifically, SEBI is responsible for:

1. Licensing and oversight — SEBI approves and regulates Asset Management Companies (AMCs), the firms that actually run mutual funds. An AMC cannot legally operate without SEBI's registration.

2. Setting rules and regulations — SEBI defines the rules that AMCs must follow: how funds must be categorized, what they can and cannot invest in, how expenses must be disclosed, and how investors must be treated.

3. Capping expense ratios — SEBI sets upper limits on how much an AMC can charge investors as an expense ratio. This protects investors from being overcharged.

4. Mandating disclosures — SEBI requires AMCs to regularly publish detailed information about their funds — portfolio holdings, NAVs, performance, and risks — so investors can make informed decisions.

5. Handling investor grievances — SEBI has mechanisms for investors to raise complaints against fund houses if they believe they've been treated unfairly.

WHY DOES SEBI EXIST?

Without a regulator, there's a risk that market participants could act in their own interest at the expense of ordinary investors — through fraud, hidden charges, or misleading information. SEBI exists to level that playing field and give investors a fair, transparent environment to participate in the markets.
