GROWTH OPTION VS IDCW OPTION (FORMERLY CALLED DIVIDEND)

When you invest in a mutual fund, you often get to choose how you want to receive any gains the fund generates. The two main choices are the Growth Option and the IDCW Option. IDCW stands for Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal — a name SEBI mandated in 2021 to replace the older term "Dividend," which was considered misleading.

THE GROWTH OPTION

In the Growth option, any profits or income earned by the fund are not paid out to you. Instead, they stay inside the fund and get reinvested. This means your NAV grows over time as the corpus compounds.

If you're investing for a long-term goal — say, retirement or a child's education — the Growth option is typically what allows your money to compound most effectively, since you're not pulling anything out along the way.

THE IDCW OPTION

In the IDCW option, the fund periodically distributes a portion of its gains (or even its capital) to investors. This payout is credited to your bank account.

Here's an important nuance: the distribution isn't "extra" money created from thin air. When a fund declares an IDCW payout, the NAV drops by that exact payout amount on the ex-dividend date. You're essentially receiving a portion of your own invested value back, or the gains that were sitting in your NAV.

IDCW can make sense if you're looking for periodic cash flows from your investment — for instance, someone in retirement who needs regular income. But it's not the same as getting a bonus on top of your investment.

TAX TREATMENT DIFFERS TOO

The Growth and IDCW options are taxed differently. IDCW payouts are added to your income and taxed at your applicable slab rate. In the Growth option, you're only taxed when you redeem (sell) your units — and the applicable capital gains tax rate depends on how long you held the investment.
